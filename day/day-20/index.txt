<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G39</span><span class="exname">Large numbers (百 / 千)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G16 (Day 7)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">八十</span> is eight tens. <span class="hz">八百</span> is eight hundreds. <span class="hz">八千</span> is eight thousands. The structure never changes — a digit in front of a unit — so once you can count to ten you can count to nine thousand.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> Put a digit in front of <span class="hz">百</span> or <span class="hz">千</span>. Chain the units from big to small. Two of anything is <span class="hz">两</span>, not <span class="hz">二</span>, when it stands in front of a unit like this.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Digit + 千 + Digit + 百 + Digit + 十 + Digit</div><div class="ex">一百 · 两百 · 八百 · 一千 · 三千 · 一千五百 · 两千八百</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">这件衣服两百块。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Zhè jiàn yīfu liǎngbǎi kuài.</span> “This piece of clothing is two hundred yuan.”</div></div>

<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G40</span><span class="exname">More / less with 多 / 少</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G10 (Day 4)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> English says “there are a lot of apples” — a noun phrase. Chinese says <span class="hz">苹果很多。</span> — “apples are many”. <span class="hz">多</span> is an <em>adjective</em>, and it goes where <span class="hz">好</span> and <span class="hz">忙</span> go.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">多</span> and <span class="hz">少</span> are adjectival predicates. They follow the thing they describe, take <span class="hz">很</span> in the plain positive, and are negated with <span class="hz">不</span> — exactly the Day-4 pattern.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Noun + 很 + 多 / 少  ·  Noun + 不 + 多 / 少</div><div class="ex">这里的苹果很多。 / 那个店的衣服不多。 / 人不少。</div></div>

<div class="grule"><div class="pat">这里的苹果很多。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Zhèli de píngguǒ hěn duō.</span> “There are a lot of apples here.”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">那个店的衣服不多。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Nàge diàn de yīfu bù duō.</span> “That shop doesn’t have many clothes.”</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">苹果很多。</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">很多苹果。</span> — the second is a noun phrase (“a lot of apples”) and needs a verb to be a sentence; on its own it is not one. <div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="exname">Track item D20 · the neutral tone in flow</span><span class="extitle">drill material from taught vocabulary only (PT-4)</span></div>
<p><b>A neutral syllable is short, light and unstressed.</b> It has no tone shape of its own; its pitch is decided by what comes before it. After a first tone it lands low-ish; after a fourth it lands very low; after a third it lands relatively high. You do not have to memorise those heights — you have to stop giving the syllable a tone.</p>

<tr><td class="mono">1st</td><td class="hz">东西</td><td class="py">dōngxi</td><td>same — yesterday’s word, same shape</td></tr>

<p><b>The particles behave the same way.</b> <span class="hz">吗</span>, <span class="hz">呢</span>, <span class="hz">的</span> and <span class="hz">们</span> are all neutral, and they are the ones most often given a tone by mistake. <span class="hz">你好吗？</span> ends light, not on a high note. <span class="hz">我们</span> is a full third tone plus a light syllable, not two equal beats.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">你好吗？ · 你呢？ · 我的 · 我们 · 他们</div><div class="ex"><b>Say it:</b> keep the final syllable of each one short. If you can clap the two syllables evenly, the second one is too heavy.</div></div>
<div class="note"><b>PS-2, stated plainly.</b> A neutral tone is <em>stored</em> — it is part of how the word is pronounced, printed unmarked in pinyin, and it never arises from a sandhi rule. That is why <span class="py">piányi</span> is written with no mark on the second syllable while <span class="py">hěn máng</span> keeps both marks even though the first is said as a rise. Sandhi changes how a marked tone sounds; neutral means there was no tone to begin with.</div>

</footer>1d:T73f,<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">八十</span> is eight tens. <span class="hz">八百</span> is eight hundreds. <span class="hz">八千</span> is eight thousands. The structure never changes — a digit in front of a unit — so once you can count to ten you can count to nine thousand.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> Put a digit in front of <span class="hz">百</span> or <span class="hz">千</span>. Chain the units from big to small. Two of anything is <span class="hz">两</span>, not <span class="hz">二</span>, when it stands in front of a unit like this.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Digit + 千 + Digit + 百 + Digit + 十 + Digit</div><div class="ex">一百 · 两百 · 八百 · 一千 · 三千 · 一千五百 · 两千八百</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">这件衣服两百块。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Zhè jiàn yīfu liǎngbǎi kuài.</span> “This piece of clothing is two hundred yuan.”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">一千块可以买十件。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Yīqiān kuài kěyǐ mǎi shí jiàn.</span> “A thousand yuan can buy ten pieces.”</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">两百</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">二百</span> — the second is heard in some regions and in formal reading, but <span class="hz">两百</span> is the default in speech, and it matches the <span class="hz">两个</span> rule you already know. And <span class="hz">十二</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">十两</span> ✗ — inside a number, twelve is always <span class="hz">二</span>.</p>

Chinese says <span class="hz">苹果很多。</span> — “apples are many”. <span class="hz">多</span> is an <em>adjective</em>, and it goes where <span class="hz">好</span> and <span class="hz">忙</span> go.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">多</span> and <span class="hz">少</span> are adjectival predicates. They follow the thing they describe, take <span class="hz">很</span> in the plain positive, and are negated with <span class="hz">不</span> — exactly the Day-4 pattern.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Noun + 很 + 多 / 少  ·  Noun + 不 + 多 / 少</div><div class="ex">这里的苹果很多。 / 那个店的衣服不多。 / 人不少。</div></div>

<div class="grule"><div class="pat">这里的苹果很多。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Zhèli de píngguǒ hěn duō.</span> “There are a lot of apples here.”</div></div>
