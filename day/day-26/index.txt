<div class="exgrid">

<div class="vc"><div class="hz">一点儿</div><div class="meta py">yīdiǎnr · Particles &amp; Function Words · <span class="mono">W.262</span> · spoken <span class="py">yìdiǎnr</span></div><div class="en">a little, a bit</div></div>

<b><span class="hz">吧</span> is neutral and light</b>, like <span class="hz">吗</span> and <span class="hz">呢</span>. It carries no tone of its own and it never takes a mark.<br/>

<b>Watch the pair <span class="hz">一点儿</span> / <span class="hz">有点儿</span>.</b> They differ by one character and by their position in the sentence, and tomorrow’s grammar section treats the difference as a rule rather than a nuance.</div></div>
<div class="warn"><b>Near-pair contrasts, taught explicitly (VI-7).</b><br/>
<b>① <span class="hz">一点儿</span> vs <span class="hz">有点儿</span>.</b> This is the most important contrast in the lesson. <span class="hz">有点儿</span> goes <em>before</em> an adjective and complains: <span class="hz">这个菜有点儿贵。</span> <span class="hz">一点儿</span> goes <em>after</em> a verb or adjective and simply measures a small amount: <span class="hz">喝一点儿水。</span> Position and attitude both differ.<br/>
<b>② <span class="hz">饭</span> vs <span class="hz">米饭</span> vs <span class="hz">早饭</span> vs <span class="hz">做饭</span>.</b> One character, four words. <span class="hz">饭</span> is a meal or rice; <span class="hz">米饭</span> is cooked rice specifically; <span class="hz">早饭</span> is breakfast; <span class="hz">做饭</span> is the verb for cooking. Learn them as a family, not four times over.<br/>
<b>③ <span class="hz">做</span> vs <span class="hz">做饭</span>.</b> <span class="hz">做</span> needs an object — <span class="hz">做什么</span>, <span class="hz">做米饭</span>. <span class="hz">做饭</span> already contains its object and stands alone: <span class="hz">我会做饭。</span> You would not say <span class="hz">我会做饭米饭</span>.<br/>
<b>④ <span class="hz">吧</span> vs <span class="hz">吗</span>.</b> Both are sentence-final and neutral. <span class="hz">吗</span> asks a genuine question; <span class="hz">吧</span> proposes something and half expects agreement. <span class="hz">我们吃饺子吗？</span> is asking; <span class="hz">我们吃饺子吧。</span> is deciding, gently.<br/>
<b>⑤ <span class="hz">面包</span> vs <span class="hz">包子</span>.</b> Same <span class="hz">包</span>, different food. Bread is baked and flat-ish; a <span class="hz">包子</span> is steamed and stuffed. The <span class="hz">面</span> in the first tells you it is a flour product.</div>10:T11dd,<p class="lead">Two points, both single words that attach to a sentence you can already build. Neither introduces a new sentence shape.</p>
<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G49</span><span class="exname">Suggestions with 吧</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G3 (Day 1)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">我们吃饺子。</span> is a statement — “we eat dumplings”. Add one light syllable and it becomes <span class="hz">我们吃饺子吧。</span> — “let’s have dumplings”. The proposal lives entirely in that syllable.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> Put <span class="hz">吧</span> at the very end of the sentence, after everything else including the object. It softens a statement into a suggestion, or a guess you expect to be confirmed.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">[complete sentence] + 吧</div><div class="ex">我们吃饺子吧。 / 我们去超市吧。 / 好吧。 / 你是学生吧。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我们今天中午吃饺子吧。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒmen jīntiān zhōngwǔ chī jiǎozi ba.</span> “Let’s have dumplings at noon today.” — the time words stay exactly where Day 9 put them; <span class="hz">吧</span> simply closes the line.</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">好吧。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Hǎo ba.</span> “All right then.” — two syllables, and the commonest way to accept a suggestion you are not thrilled about.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">我们吃饺子吗？</span> ✓ asks whether you eat dumplings; <span class="hz">我们吃饺子吧。</span> ✓ proposes eating them. Same sentence, two different particles, two different social acts. And <span class="hz">吧饺子吃</span> ✗ — <span class="hz">吧</span> cannot move; it is final, always.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Your fourth sentence-final particle.</b> <span class="hz">吗</span> (Day 1), <span class="hz">呢</span> (Day 2), <span class="hz">吧</span> (today) — and all three are neutral, short and unmarked in pinyin. They are the smallest words in Chinese and they carry the social weight of a sentence.</div>

<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G50</span><span class="exname">一点儿 (a little, after V / Adj)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G42 (Day 21)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> Day 21 gave you <span class="hz">一些</span> for a vague plural — <span class="hz">一些书</span>, some books. <span class="hz">一点儿</span> does the same job for a small <em>amount</em>: a bit of water, a little rice. Countable things take <span class="hz">一些</span>; amounts take <span class="hz">一点儿</span>.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">一点儿</span> follows the verb and precedes the noun. It can also stand alone after a verb when the noun is obvious.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Verb + 一点儿 + Noun  ·  Verb + 一点儿</div><div class="ex">喝一点儿水 · 吃一点儿水果 · 我会做一点儿 · 我想吃一点儿</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我想吃一点儿水果。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ xiǎng chī yīdiǎnr shuǐguǒ.</span> Said <span class="py">Wó xiǎng chī yìdiǎnr shuí guǒ.</span> — “I’d like a bit of fruit.”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我会做一点儿。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ huì zuò yīdiǎnr.</span> “I can cook a little.” — no noun at all; the thing being cooked is understood.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">喝一点儿水</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">一点儿喝水</span> ✗ — <span class="hz">一点儿</span> follows the verb, never precedes it. And the pair that matters most: <span class="hz">这个菜有点儿贵。</span> ✓ (before an adjective, a complaint) vs <span class="hz">吃一点儿菜。</span> ✓ (after a verb, a quantity). Swap them and both sentences break.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Two words, one memory hook.</b> <span class="hz">有点儿</span> starts with <span class="hz">有</span> and comes <em>before</em> what it modifies. <span class="hz">一点儿</span> starts with <span class="hz">一</span> and comes <em>after</em>. If you can remember which one leads, the rest follows.</div>
