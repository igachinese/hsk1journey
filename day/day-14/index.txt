<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G30</span><span class="exname">Verb + 到 (until / reach)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G28 (Day 13)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">我工作。</span> → <span class="hz">我工作到五点。</span> The clock time has moved <em>behind</em> the verb — the only time all course it has been allowed to.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">到</span> placed after a verb marks the <b>end point</b>: how far the action runs, in time or in space. What follows <span class="hz">到</span> is that end point — a clock time, or a place.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Subject + (start time) + Verb + 到 + End point</div><div class="ex">我工作到五点。 · 我睡到七点。 · 我八点到五点上班。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我工作到五点。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ gōngzuò dào wǔ diǎn.</span> “I work until five.”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我明天七点到公司。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ míngtiān qī diǎn dào gōngsī.</span> “I arrive at the company at seven tomorrow.” — here <span class="hz">到</span> is the main verb, “arrive”, not the end-point marker.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">我工作到五点。</span> ✓ (end point, after the verb) vs <span class="hz">我到五点工作。</span> ✗ · and <span class="hz">我五点下班。</span> ✓ (a plain time-when, before the verb, G24). Both orders exist and mean different things — <span class="hz">到</span> is what licenses the second position.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Two jobs, one character.</b> <span class="hz">到</span> is a verb (“arrive”) and an end-point marker (“until”). You can tell them apart by what sits in front: if there is another verb, <span class="hz">到</span> is marking that verb’s end point. <span class="hz">我到公司。</span> = I arrive. <span class="hz">我工作到五点。</span> = I work until five.<br/>
<b>The span frame:</b> put a start and an end together and you get a whole shift — <span class="hz">我八点到五点上班。</span> “I’m at work from eight to five.”</div>

<div class="exhead"><span class="exname">Track item D14 · e / o finals</span><span class="extitle">drill material from taught vocabulary only (PT-4)</span></div>
<p><b>The <span class="mono">e</span> that isn’t “eh”.</b> The single final <span class="mono">-e</span> is not the vowel of English “bed”. The tongue sits mid and <em>back</em>, the lips are relaxed and unrounded, and the sound is closer to the middle of English “the” or the vowel in “duh”. If your lips round, you have said <span class="mono">o</span> instead.</p>
