<div class="warn"><b>Near-pair contrasts, taught explicitly (VI-7).</b><br/>

<b>④ <span class="hz">了</span> vs <span class="hz">吧</span> vs <span class="hz">呢</span> vs <span class="hz">吗</span>.</b> Four small syllables at or near the end of a sentence. <span class="hz">吗</span> asks, <span class="hz">吧</span> proposes, <span class="hz">呢</span> hands the question back, and <span class="hz">了</span> — alone among them — is not a question particle at all. <div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G51</span><span class="exname">Completed action with 了</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: hard · prerequisites: G15 · G24 (both Day 9)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">我看电影。</span> is what I do, or what I am going to do. <span class="hz">我看了电影。</span> is what I have done. One syllable turns an activity into a completed event.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">了</span> goes directly after the verb. To negate, use <span class="hz">没</span> before the verb and <b>drop <span class="hz">了</span> entirely</b> — this is the part learners get wrong for months, so get it right now.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Subject + Verb + 了 (+ Object)  ·  negative: Subject + 没 + Verb</div><div class="ex">我看了电影。 / 我没看电影。 · 我吃了午饭。 / 我没吃午饭。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我看了一个电影。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ kàn le yī ge diànyǐng.</span> “I watched a film.” — the measure word from Day 11 sits where it always has; <span class="hz">了</span> attaches to the verb, not to the sentence.</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我没吃晚饭。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ méi chī wǎnfàn.</span> “I didn’t have dinner.” — no <span class="hz">了</span>, because a negated event never completed.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">我没看电影。</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">我没看了电影。</span> ✗ — the two cannot co-occur. And <span class="hz">我不看电影。</span> ✓ is different again: <span class="hz">不</span> is “I don’t / won’t watch films”, a habit or a refusal; <span class="hz">没</span> is “I didn’t”, a report about the past.</p>
<div class="note"><b>The one word of honesty this point deserves.</b> <span class="hz">了</span> does more in real Chinese than “past tense”, and it is not a past tense — it marks completion and change, which is why <span class="hz">我知道了</span> means “ah, now I understand”. At HSK 1 you need the completed-action use and the <span class="hz">没</span> negation. The rest arrives later, and the habit that will hurt you is not under-using <span class="hz">了</span> but stapling it to every past-tense verb.</div>
</div>
</div>
<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G52</span><span class="exname">Ongoing action with 正在</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G32 (Day 15)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> Day 15 gave you <span class="hz">在</span> before a place: <span class="hz">他在家工作。</span> Put <span class="hz">在</span> before a verb instead and it stops meaning “at” and starts meaning “in the middle of”. <span class="hz">正在</span> is the fuller, clearer form of exactly that.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">正在</span> goes before the verb. It answers “what are you doing right now?”</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Subject + 正在 + Verb (+ Object)</div><div class="ex">他正在看电视。 / 我正在做饭。 / 孩子们正在玩。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">他正在看电视。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Tā zhèngzài kàn diànshì.</span> “He’s watching television (right now).”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我正在做饭，你呢？</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ zhèngzài zuòfàn, nǐ ne?</span> “I’m cooking — and you?” — yesterday’s verb, today’s frame, and the <span class="hz">呢</span>-question that §4 drills.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">他正在看电视。</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">他看电视正在。</span> ✗ — <span class="hz">正在</span> leads the verb and cannot trail it. And against today’s other point: <span class="hz">我正在看电影。</span> (mid-film) vs <span class="hz">我看了电影。</span> (film finished). Before the verb, in progress; after the verb, complete.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Why the two live on the same day.</b> They are opposite ends of one idea — where an action stands in time — and teaching them together lets the contrast do the work that two separate days would each have to do alone. The cost is that today is conceptually dense, which is exactly why the battery profile is a recognition ramp rather than a production sprint.</div>

<div class="grule"><div class="pat">你呢？ · 他呢？ · 晚饭呢？</div><div class="ex"><b>Say it:</b> three short <span class="hz">呢</span>-questions. <div class="grule"><div class="pat">我吃了午饭，你呢？ —— 我没吃。</div><div class="ex"><b>Say it:</b> the same rhythm carrying today’s grammar. Note the answer is two syllables and still falls.</div></div>
<p><b>One honest complication.</b> A <span class="hz">呢</span>-question with a longer phrase in front of it will often carry a small pause before the particle — <span class="hz">晚饭 · 呢？</span> Both the pausing and the smooth versions are ordinary. What is not ordinary is putting a rising English question-mark contour across the whole sentence, which flattens every tone in it.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Nothing here changes a stored value.</b> Sentence contour is an overlay on top of the tones, not a replacement for them, so the manifest’s question rows are deliberately <em>unsplit</em>: written and spoken columns agree.
