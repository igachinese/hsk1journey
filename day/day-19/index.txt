<div class="warn"><b>Near-pair contrasts, taught explicitly (VI-7).</b><br/>
<b>① <span class="hz">买</span> <span class="py">mǎi</span> vs <span class="hz">卖</span> <span class="py">mài</span>.</b> Third tone against fourth. <span class="hz">我买苹果。</span> = I buy apples (money leaves me). <span class="hz">我卖苹果。</span> = I sell apples (money comes to me). <div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G35</span><span class="exname">Serial verbs: 去 + Place + Verb</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisites: G14 (Day 5) · G31 (Day 15)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> Since Day 14 you could say <span class="hz">我去超市。</span> — I go to the supermarket. Today: <span class="hz">我去超市买东西。</span> — I go to the supermarket <em>to buy things</em>. Two verbs, one subject, one trip.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> Put the second verb phrase straight after the place. No word joins them — no “to”, no “and”. The order carries the meaning: you go first, then you do the thing, and Chinese states them in that order.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Subject + 去 + Place + Verb (+ Object)</div><div class="ex">我去超市买东西。 · 我去商店买衣服。 · 我去学校上课。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我去超市买东西。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ qù chāoshì mǎi dōngxi.</span> “I’m going to the supermarket to buy things.”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">她去医院看病。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Tā qù yīyuàn kànbìng.</span> “She’s going to the hospital to see a doctor.”</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">我去超市买东西。</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">我买东西去超市。</span> ✗ — reversing the order is not a stylistic choice, it is wrong. And compare Day 15’s <span class="hz">我在超市买东西。</span> — that says where the buying happens; today’s says you are <em>going there</em> in order to do it.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Why this waited.</b> The pattern needs a place worth travelling to and a verb worth doing there. Until today the course had <span class="hz">学校</span>, <span class="hz">医院</span> and <span class="hz">公司</span> but no shops and no <span class="hz">买</span>. Now it has all of them, and the pattern pays for itself immediately.</div>

<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G38</span><span class="exname">Money &amp; prices (块 / 元 / 钱; 多少钱)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisites: G16 (Day 7) · G25 (Day 11)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">多少</span> asks for an open count — you learned that on Day 11 for phone numbers. Put <span class="hz">钱</span> after it and you are asking a price.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> Ask with <span class="hz">多少钱？</span>. Answer with <b>Number + 块</b>, optionally followed by <span class="hz">钱</span>. The measure word behaves exactly like <span class="hz">个</span> and <span class="hz">件</span>: it stands between the number and what is counted.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">(Thing) + 多少钱？  ·  Number + 块 (+ 钱)  ·  Number + 元</div><div class="ex">这个多少钱？ —— 三块。 / 三块钱。 / 八十元。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">这件衣服多少钱？</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Zhè jiàn yīfu duōshao qián?</span> “How much is this piece of clothing?”</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">一块钱一个。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Yī kuài qián yī ge.</span> “One yuan each.” — the price first, then what it buys.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">多少钱？</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">几钱？</span> ✗ — a price has no small ceiling, so it takes <span class="hz">多少</span>, never <span class="hz">几</span>. And <span class="hz">三块</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">三钱</span> ✗ — you count with the measure word, not with the noun.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Your number range is the limit.</b> Day 7 gave you zero to ninety-nine, so today you can price anything up to <span class="hz">九十九块</span>. Hundreds and thousands arrive tomorrow, and the shopping gets more expensive.</div>

<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="exname">Track item D19 · third tone before a non-third</span><span class="extitle">drill material from taught vocabulary only (PT-4)</span></div>
<p><b>The third tone usually does not rise.</b> You learned it as a dip-and-rise: down, then up. That full shape only survives when the syllable stands alone or ends a phrase. In front of any <em>other</em> tone, the rise is dropped and what is left is a low, flat, slightly creaky syllable — the <b>half-third</b>.</p>

<p><b>Today’s pair is the reason this matters.</b> <span class="hz">买</span> <span class="py">mǎi</span> and <span class="hz">卖</span> <span class="py">mài</span> are one tone apart, and <span class="hz">买</span> is the one that changes shape depending on where it sits. On its own — <span class="hz">我买。</span> — it dips and rises. In front of another word — <span class="hz">买东西</span> — it stays low and flat. Neither of those is <span class="hz">卖</span>, which drops from high.</p>
<div class="note"><b>PS-3 review, inside this item.</b> There is one case where the third tone does something else entirely: in front of <em>another third tone</em>, it becomes a full second tone. <span class="hz">你好</span> is said <span class="py">ní hǎo</span>. That is the sandhi rule from Day 2, and it is a different rule from the half-third. Two thirds → the first becomes a rise. A third before anything else → the first goes low and flat.</div>
