<div class="warn"><b>Near-pair contrasts, taught explicitly (VI-7).</b><br/>
<b>① <span class="hz">坐</span> vs <span class="hz">做</span>.</b> Both <span class="py">zuò</span>, both fourth tone, entirely different meanings. <span class="hz">我做饭</span> I cook; <span class="hz">我坐车</span> I take the car. <b>② <span class="hz">开</span> vs <span class="hz">开车</span>.</b> <span class="hz">开</span> needs an object — <span class="hz">开电视</span>, <span class="hz">开车</span>. <span class="hz">开车</span> already contains its object and stands alone: <span class="hz">我会开车。</span> The same relationship as <span class="hz">做</span> and <span class="hz">做饭</span> on Day 26.<br/>
<b>③ <span class="hz">怎么</span> vs <span class="hz">怎么样</span>.</b> <span class="hz">怎么</span> + verb asks method: <span class="hz">怎么去？</span> <span class="hz">怎么样</span> stands at the end and asks for an assessment: <span class="hz">这个电影怎么样？</span><br/>
<b>④ <span class="hz">来</span> vs <span class="hz">去</span>.</b> Both have been yours for weeks — <span class="hz">去</span> since Day 14 and <span class="hz">来</span> since Day 17 — but today’s grammar point is the first time the choice between them is stated as a rule. It depends entirely on where the speaker is.<br/>
<b>⑤ <span class="hz">回</span> vs <span class="hz">去</span>.</b> <span class="hz">去学校</span> is going to school; <span class="hz">回学校</span> is going back to a school you had already left. <span class="hz">回</span> always implies a return.</div>10:T19d0,<p class="lead">Three points — the cap, and the reason today’s battery is a transform spine. <div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G55</span><span class="exname">坐 + transport (travel by)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G14 (Day 5)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> <span class="hz">坐</span> on its own means to sit. Put a vehicle after it and it stops meaning sitting and starts meaning travelling: <span class="hz">坐火车</span> is “by train”, not “sit on a train”.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">坐</span> + vehicle goes <em>before</em> the main verb. The travelling is the method; the verb that follows is the point.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">Subject + 坐 + Vehicle + Verb (+ Place)</div><div class="ex">我坐火车去。 / 我坐出租车回家。 / 他坐飞机来。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我坐出租车去电影院。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒ zuò chūzūchē qù diànyǐngyuàn.</span> “I’m taking a taxi to the cinema.” — method first, then the going, then the destination.</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">我们坐火车回家吧。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Wǒmen zuò huǒchē huí jiā ba.</span> “Let’s take the train home.” — with Day 26’s suggestion particle closing the line.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">我坐火车去。</span> ✓ vs <span class="hz">我去坐火车。</span> ✓ — both are correct and they mean different things. The first is “I’ll go by train”; the second is “I’m going in order to catch a train”. Word order carries the whole difference.</p>
<p><b>And the exception you should know:</b> driving yourself is <span class="hz">开车</span>, not <span class="hz">坐车</span>. <span class="hz">我开车去。</span> = I’ll drive. <span class="hz">我坐车去。</span> = I’ll go by car, and somebody else is driving.</p>

<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G56</span><span class="exname">怎么 + Verb (how to …)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G13 (Day 5)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> You have been asking <span class="hz">什么</span>, <span class="hz">谁</span>, <span class="hz">哪儿</span> and <span class="hz">怎么样</span> for weeks. <span class="hz">怎么</span> is the one that asks about <em>method</em>, and it goes straight in front of the verb.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">怎么</span> + verb. Like every Chinese question word, it sits where the answer would sit — you do not move anything else.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">(Subject +) 怎么 + Verb (+ Object)</div><div class="ex">怎么去？ / 你怎么去学校？ / 这个菜怎么做？ / 怎么说？</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">你怎么去电影院？ —— 我坐出租车去。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Nǐ zěnme qù diànyǐngyuàn? — Wǒ zuò chūzūchē qù.</span> Question and answer share their shape exactly; only <span class="hz">怎么</span> is swapped for the method.</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">这个字怎么读？</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Zhège zì zěnme dú?</span> “How is this character read?” — using <span class="hz">字</span> and <span class="hz">读</span> from Days 16 and 21.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> <span class="hz">怎么去？</span> ✓ asks by what means; <span class="hz">去哪儿？</span> ✓ asks the destination; <span class="hz">怎么样？</span> ✓ asks for an opinion. Three question forms, three different answers, and the difference is worth ten minutes of your attention because learners confuse the first and third for months.</p>
<div class="note"><b>The answer slots straight in.</b> Ask <span class="hz">你怎么去？</span> and the reply is <span class="hz">我坐火车去。</span> — the method takes exactly the position <span class="hz">怎么</span> occupied. This is the single most reliable feature of Chinese question words and it has held since Day 3.</div>

<div class="card learn">
<div class="exhead"><span class="pill">G57</span><span class="exname">来 / 去 (come / go)</span><span class="extitle">difficulty: core · prerequisite: G35 (Day 19)</span></div>
<div class="exbody">
<p><b>Notice first.</b> You have used both words for weeks without ever being told how to choose. The rule is short: it depends on where the speaker is standing.</p>
<p><b>The rule.</b> <span class="hz">来</span> is movement <em>toward</em> the speaker; <span class="hz">去</span> is movement <em>away</em>. The same journey takes different verbs depending on who is describing it.</p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">来 + Place (toward me)  ·  去 + Place (away from me)</div><div class="ex">你来我家。 / 我去你家。 · 他明天来。 / 我明天去。</div></div>
<p><b>Two examples:</b></p>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">你来我家吧，我做饭。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Nǐ lái wǒ jiā ba, wǒ zuòfàn.</span> “Come to my place, I’ll cook.” — I am at my home, so the movement is toward me: <span class="hz">来</span>.</div></div>
<div class="grule"><div class="pat">明天我去你家。</div><div class="ex"><span class="py">Míngtiān wǒ qù nǐ jiā.</span> “I’ll come to your place tomorrow.” — English says “come”, Chinese says <span class="hz">去</span>, because the movement is away from where I am now.</div></div>
<p><b>Minimal contrast (GE-4):</b> that last pair is the whole point. English lets you say “I’ll come to your place”; Chinese does not, because you are not moving toward yourself. <span class="hz">我来你家</span> ✗ unless you are already there. This is the commonest interference error English speakers make in Chinese, and you have met it on the last teaching day, which is exactly when you can afford to notice it.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Why this waited until Day 29.</b> Both words have been available since Days 14 and 17, used in contexts where only one was possible. The rule needs a speaker, a listener and two places to be worth stating — and it needs <span class="hz">回</span> alongside it to be complete. Today you have all of that.</div>
